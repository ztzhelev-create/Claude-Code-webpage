Strawberries are available for most of the year and worth eating for about two
weeks of it. The rest are grown for the journey, not the eating.

When the good ones arrive, the temptation is to do something with them. Resist it
for the first few days. A ripe strawberry at room temperature, hulled and eaten
over the sink, is the whole point, and no tart improves on it.

After that, the best things to do are the laziest. Sliced strawberries left with a
spoonful of sugar for twenty minutes make their own syrup. A little black pepper
or a splash of balsamic makes them taste more like themselves, not less.

Keep them out of the fridge if you are eating them that day — cold flattens the
smell, and most of a strawberry is smell. Rinse them with the hulls still on, so
they do not fill with water.

Then the fortnight ends, and you wait.
